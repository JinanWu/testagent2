# Busy-reader publication pattern

Use this pattern when the user wants AI Lab News to feel like a real publication rather than a short summary.

## Goal

Make the piece readable in about 5 minutes for busy colleagues.

## Structure

1. Title
   - Clear topic framing, not just a source title.
2. One-sentence summary
   - State the main takeaway immediately.
3. Section 1: What happened / what problem this reflects
   - Explain the concrete change or tension.
4. Section 2: Why it matters to the team
   - Connect to workflow, governance, deployment, review, or operational risk.
5. Section 3: What to do or how to interpret it
   - Give a practical reading or action path.
6. Closing conclusion
   - Reduce the whole issue to one memorable sentence.

## Style notes

- Keep the prose narrative; do not make it look like a source dump.
- Merge related updates into a single editorial signal when they point to the same trend.
- If the topic is too narrow or too technical, reframe it around the work impact.
- Preserve source URLs in the body or a dedicated source section.
- Use transitions so the article feels like a publication, not a note.

## Common pitfall

A response can be technically correct but still fail if it reads like a list of annotations. Prefer a cohesive article voice whenever the user asks for a "刊物" or "試產" version.
