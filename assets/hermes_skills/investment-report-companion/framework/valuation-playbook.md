# Valuation Playbook

Valuation is not a mechanical multiplication exercise. The assistant must explain why the chosen method and multiple are appropriate.

## PER

Formula:

> Target Price = Forecast EPS × Target PER

Use when earnings are reasonably interpretable.

Always test:

- Why this forecast year?
- Is EPS normalized?
- Why this target multiple?
- What is the historical range?
- What is the peer range?
- Is growth structural, cyclical, or temporary?
- Does the stock already price in future earnings?

### Required scenario range

When the user questions valuation, calculate a range rather than defend a single target.

Example:

| Scenario | EPS | PER | Implied price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 8.59 | 18x | 155 |
| Reasonable | 8.59 | 20x | 172 |
| Broker case | 8.59 | 23x | 198 |

Then ask what evidence is needed to justify the higher multiple.

## PBR

Formula:

> Target Price = Forecast BVPS × Target PBR

Use when balance-sheet strength, asset value, and cycle position matter.

Especially relevant for:

- shipping;
- financials;
- asset-heavy cyclicals.

Always test:

- historical PBR range;
- ROE;
- asset quality;
- cycle position;
- downside protection;
- dividend yield;
- whether current EPS is inflated or depressed.

## EV/EBITDA

Use when depreciation or capital intensity makes net income less informative.

Check:

- maintenance CAPEX;
- growth CAPEX;
- debt;
- lease liabilities;
- cyclicality;
- EBITDA quality.

## SOTP / NAV

Use for holding companies, conglomerates, and asset plays.

Required steps:

1. list each subsidiary or asset;
2. assign valuation method;
3. state assumptions;
4. deduct debt;
5. apply holding-company discount where appropriate;
6. calculate per-share NAV.

Do not present NAV as objective truth. It is an assumption stack.

## DCF

Use only when long-term cash-flow assumptions are defensible.

Always state:

- revenue growth assumptions;
- margin assumptions;
- WACC;
- terminal growth rate;
- sensitivity range.

## Valuation Red Flags

- using peak-cycle EPS with a low PER;
- using distant forecast-year EPS without discounting execution risk;
- raising target PER without explaining why;
- treating optionality as if it were realized earnings;
- using PBR without checking ROE;
- ignoring customer concentration;
- ignoring CAPEX and depreciation;
- ignoring supply expansion.
