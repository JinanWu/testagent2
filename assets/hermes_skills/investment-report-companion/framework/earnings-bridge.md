# Earnings Bridge

The assistant must convert report language into an earnings transmission chain.

## Core Formula

> Revenue = Volume × ASP × Product Mix × FX × Capacity × Market Share

> Gross Profit = Revenue × Gross Margin

> Operating Profit = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses

> EPS = Operating Profit + Non-operating Items − Tax − Minority Interest, adjusted for share count

## What to Identify

For each major earnings revision, identify whether the driver is:

- volume growth;
- ASP increase;
- product-mix improvement;
- utilization-rate improvement;
- operating leverage;
- raw-material pressure;
- FX;
- new capacity;
- depreciation;
- one-off profit;
- low-base effect;
- customer concentration;
- acquisition effect.

## Interpretation Labels

Tag each earnings driver as one of:

- structural;
- cyclical;
- temporary;
- accounting-driven;
- unverified optionality.

## Examples

### Cooling supplier

> AI server power consumption rises  
> → air cooling shifts toward liquid cooling  
> → cold plate, manifold, rack, CDU demand rises  
> → liquid-cooling revenue mix increases  
> → product mix improves  
> → gross margin and EPS rise

### Semiconductor equipment supplier expansion

> Customer orders rise  
> → supplier expands capacity  
> → hiring, training, and low initial utilization raise cost  
> → short-term gross margin falls  
> → revenue rises faster than EPS  
> → target price may be revised down despite stronger revenue

### EMS / ODM supplier

> AI server rack shipments rise  
> → Buy & Sell model lowers gross-margin percentage  
> → scale raises absolute gross profit and operating profit  
> → EPS still rises  
> → market may rerate the stock

## Common Misreads

Avoid these mistakes:

- assuming higher revenue automatically means better earnings quality;
- assuming lower gross margin automatically means worse business quality;
- ignoring operating leverage;
- mixing recurring profit with disposal gains;
- treating low-base recovery as structural growth;
- ignoring depreciation after CAPEX expansion.
