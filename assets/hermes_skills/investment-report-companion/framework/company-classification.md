# Company Classification

Before discussing valuation, classify the stock. The correct valuation lens depends on the economic structure of the business.

## A. Stable Compounder

Typical signals:

- recurring demand;
- stable margins;
- moderate but durable growth;
- relatively predictable cash flow;
- consistent reinvestment runway.

Primary questions:

- Can ROE remain high without excessive leverage?
- Is growth durable rather than temporary?
- Does valuation assume an unrealistic terminal growth rate?

Useful methods:

- PER;
- DCF;
- EV/EBITDA where appropriate.

## B. Cyclical Stock

Typical signals:

- freight rates;
- commodity spreads;
- inventory cycles;
- utilization rates;
- capacity additions;
- volatile EPS.

Primary questions:

- Is the company near peak, trough, or normalization?
- Is current EPS sustainable?
- Is low PER an illusion caused by peak earnings?
- Does PBR or dividend yield offer downside protection?

Useful methods:

- PBR;
- normalized EPS;
- dividend yield;
- balance-sheet analysis.

## C. Structural Growth Stock

Typical signals:

- specification upgrades;
- rising penetration;
- new platforms;
- market-share gains;
- product-mix improvement.

Primary questions:

- Is adoption measurable?
- Is growth driven by volume, ASP, mix, or share gain?
- Is gross-margin expansion sustainable?
- Has the market already priced in the growth curve?

Useful methods:

- PER;
- PEG as a secondary lens;
- scenario analysis.

## D. Project-Based / Optionality Stock

Typical signals:

- qualification;
- sampling;
- NRE;
- pilot production;
- new customer project;
- uncertain ramp.

Primary questions:

- Which project stage has actually been reached?
- What revenue and EPS contribution is visible?
- Is the market paying for revenue that is not yet realized?

Project stage ladder:

1. concept;
2. qualification;
3. sampling;
4. design win;
5. order;
6. pilot production;
7. mass production;
8. meaningful revenue contribution;
9. visible EPS contribution.

## E. Financial Stock

Primary questions:

- How much earnings comes from banking, insurance, securities, or one-off gains?
- Is ROE improving?
- Is BVPS stable?
- Are credit costs and capital ratios healthy?

Useful methods:

- PBR;
- ROE;
- adjusted profit analysis.

## F. Holding Company / Asset Stock

Primary questions:

- What is the value of each subsidiary or asset?
- What debt should be deducted?
- What holding-company discount is appropriate?

Useful methods:

- SOTP;
- NAV;
- discount-to-NAV.

## G. Turnaround Stock

Typical signals:

- low base;
- capacity restructuring;
- cost reduction;
- demand recovery;
- loss narrowing.

Primary questions:

- Is growth a genuine structural upgrade or just recovery from a weak base?
- Does operating leverage work in both directions?
- Has the market already priced in recovery?
